00

The collapse has a measured cause, and it is not a penalty

New finding — neither source document had this evidence

Both Wix sitemaps are machine-readable. The SEO plan recorded them as unparseable and left completeness as an open release gate; they only fail through a rendering browser, because Wix serves them as raw XML. Fetched directly they parse cleanly. That closes the gate: the legacy estate is 187 real URLs, plus five Wix template demo pages (/fm/villain-showdown-finale and siblings) that were never EntireFM content.

This is the whole story. Google has not demoted the site — three-quarters of the pages that earned the enquiries stopped existing. A 404 sheds everything the URL had accumulated. The enquiries stopped because the landing pages stopped.

01

The restored pages are 74% identical to each other

Amends SEO Plan §4.3 — "what makes a location page genuinely local"

This is the single largest risk to the recovery, and it is a risk the rebuild creates rather than inherits. The plan is explicit that location pages must not be built by city-name swapping. The implementation does exactly that.

Every paired city variant sits near 88% identical. The differentiating content is the city name substituted into otherwise fixed sentences — including mid-sentence, where it reads as keyword insertion rather than English: "single-source facilities management manchester for commercial property owners".

Publishing 187 URLs in this state converts a recoverable technical problem into a quality problem, which is far harder to reverse. The 404s cost rankings; a site-wide doorway-page assessment costs the domain.

Recommendation: restore all 187, index them in tiers

URL restoration and indexation are separate decisions, and the plan conflates them. Serve every legacy URL a 200 immediately — that recovers the links and the history. Then earn indexation:

  • Tier 1 — the cities that actually produced enquiries. Genuinely written, not generated. Local operating conditions, named districts and estates, real nearby proof, city-specific FAQs. Target under 30% overlap with any sibling. Index at launch.
  • Tier 2 — secondary cities. Index once each page carries at least one piece of evidence that exists nowhere else on the site. Batch of five to eight per week is a realistic rate for content anyone would actually read.
  • Tier 3 — the long tail and near-duplicate variants. 200 and reachable, noindex until differentiated. A noindex page still passes links and still converts a visitor who lands on it.

Tier assignment should come from Search Console history, not intuition — see §06.

Test before publishing, not after

Add a similarity gate to the QA suite: fail the build when any two indexable pages exceed a set overlap. It is the only way a 187-page estate stays honest as it grows, and it makes the plan's "no city-name swapping" rule enforceable rather than aspirational.

02

There is no structured data anywhere

Gap in both documents — neither mentions schema

Zero JSON-LD blocks across all 235 built pages. For a business whose demand is local and service-shaped, this is a large, cheap, entirely mechanical win — and it is the layer that decides whether the site is legible to AI answer engines (§05).

  • Organization once, sitewide, with real registration and contact detail.
  • ProfessionalService per location page, with genuine areaServed — not a copied national radius.
  • Service per service page, tied back to the organisation.
  • FAQPage on the pages that carry real FAQs — the templates already have the section.
  • BreadcrumbList everywhere, which also forces the internal-link hierarchy to be decided.
  • Article on the restored /post/* pages, with original publication dates preserved.

Preserving the original dates matters: these posts have history, and re-dating them to the migration date throws it away for no gain.

03

Neither plan says how to perform the cutover

Missing from SEO Plan §11 — the roadmap starts after this step

The roadmap covers what to build and what to do in the first 90 days, but not the switch itself. The site being replaced is live and already damaged, so the cutover carries real risk and deserves a written sequence.

  1. Freeze the current site. No further URL changes to production until cutover. Every change now adds a second migration to unpick.
  2. Export Search Console first. Sixteen months of landing pages and queries, before anything moves. This data is the tier assignment, the redirect-direction evidence, and the only baseline against which recovery can be measured. It cannot be recovered later.
  3. Verify against staging. npm run verify:legacy-estate:prod pointed at the staging host — all 187 must return 200 before DNS moves.
  4. Confirm the indexing gate. The build already blocks crawling anywhere other than the canonical host. Verify it releases correctly on the production host, and that staging never became indexable.
  5. Cut over, then submit. Sitemap resubmission and manual inspection of the twenty highest-value legacy URLs on day one.
  6. Watch daily for fourteen days. Search Console Pages, crawl stats, and server 404 logs. Any legacy path returning a 404 post-cutover is a release defect.
  7. Keep a rollback. The current deployment stays revertible for the full window. Define in advance what triggers a revert, so it is not argued about under pressure.

Do not wait for design

The instinct to launch once it looks finished is the expensive one here. Each week of 404s is another week of decay on 138 URLs. A plain, fast, correctly-routed site restores the estate; design improves it afterwards, at no SEO cost. Structure first is the right call — hold that line.

04

The local entity layer is absent from the strategy

Gap in SEO Plan §4 — treats geography as purely on-page

The plan builds the geographic estate entirely from page content. For a business chasing city-level commercial intent, roughly half of local visibility sits outside the website altogether, and none of it is mentioned:

  • Google Business Profile — the primary local entity. Categories, service areas, and posts, kept current.
  • Name, address and phone consistency across the site and every external directory. The rebuild has a verified-contact config; that same record should be the single source everywhere.
  • Citations and trade registers — the FM and building-services directories that carry real referral weight, not volume link-building.
  • Reviews, which influence local ranking directly and are the proof the location pages currently lack most.

This matters for a second reason: it is the cheapest source of the genuine local evidence §01 requires. Real reviews and real named local work are exactly the material that makes a city page differentiated rather than generated.

05

The two documents should meet at answer-engine visibility

Bridges AI Blueprint §13 and SEO Plan §6

The AI Blueprint is about running the business with AI. The SEO plan is about ranking in Google. Neither addresses being cited by AI assistants — which is where a growing share of FM procurement research now begins, and where a mid-market operator can outrank its size.

The Blueprint's public tools — the compliance checker, the PPM planner, the cost benchmark — are treated there as a product-led funnel. They are also the strongest link-earning and citation-earning assets in either plan, and the SEO plan under-weights them badly. A tool that returns a genuinely useful answer gets referenced; a service page does not.

What this needs concretely:

  • Answer the question in the first paragraph, then support it. Extractable structure beats narrative build-up for both featured snippets and AI citation.
  • A stated position on AI crawlers, made deliberately. Blocking them protects nothing here and removes the site from the answers.
  • The Compliance Centre distinction the Blueprint already specifies — legal requirement versus guidance versus typical practice — is exactly what makes content quotable and safe to cite. It belongs in the SEO plan's §6.2 as a content requirement, not just a product spec.
  • Schema (§02) is the mechanical half of this. Entity-clear pages are the ones that get resolved and cited correctly.
06

Two release gates are still open

Amends SEO Plan §10.3 — sitemap gate now closed, two remain

The sitemap half of the plan's release gate is closed and evidenced. Two inputs remain, and both require account access this work cannot reach:

  • Search Console history — sixteen months of landing pages and queries. The only source that reveals URLs which earned traffic without appearing in any sitemap, and the only basis for ranking recovery by measured value rather than inference.
  • Backlink data — which legacy URLs are externally linked. A 404 on a linked page wastes the link entirely; these deserve priority regardless of their own traffic.

Until both are loaded, three decisions are being made on inference rather than evidence: the indexation tiers in §01, the redirect direction for each city pair, and which of the 35 surviving URLs are worth protecting hardest. None of that blocks the cutover — coverage is complete either way — but it does mean the sequencing is provisional, and it should be revisited the week the data arrives.
